Item writing

Too long to be wrong?

“Too long to be wrong.”

In poorly constructed multiple-choice items, the key may sometimes be slightly longer, more specific, or more carefully worded than the distractors. A test-wise candidate may detect that pattern and choose the answer without demonstrating the reading ability the item was intended to measure. That is an unintended clue, and it can introduce construct-irrelevant variance.

Test design

Does the order of test items matter?

Test design is not only about what we ask. It is also about when we ask it. Even something as seemingly simple as item order can introduce construct-irrelevant variance and affect the validity and fairness of score interpretations. There may be no universally “correct” test order, but there should be a reason for the order you choose.
